## 1. Constitutional purpose and jurisdiction

This Constitution establishes the rules of a transcontinental civic framework for:
1. protecting human rights in international trade and cross-border commercial relationships;
2. governing algorithmic systems used within the framework;
3. defining standards for the creation, verification, exchange, licensing, valuation, and distribution of logic and other logical assets.

It is not a claim to establish a world government, replace states, or exercise public authority over people or territories. It binds the Institute and participating entities through their own constitutive instruments, contracts, membership rules, and voluntary adoption. It acquires additional legal force only where and to the extent competent jurisdictions or authorized parties adopt it through valid legal instruments.

## 2. Rights floor

Every participating institution, company, agent system, marketplace, and service must respect:
- equal dignity and non-discrimination;
- privacy, data protection, and control over personal information;
- freedom of thought, expression, association, and peaceful participation;
- fair working conditions, freedom from forced and child labour, and worker representation;
- consumer protection, truthful disclosure, and protection against deceptive or coercive practices;
- access to remedy, explanation, correction, appeal, and human review;
- accessibility and reasonable accommodation;
- due process before adverse decisions affecting a person or organization;
- protection against arbitrary exclusion, surveillance, manipulation, and retaliation.

No commercial benefit, algorithmic score, efficiency target, or proprietary interest overrides this rights floor.

## 3. International trade safeguards

Before a participating entity launches a material cross-border product, service, supply relationship, or trade agreement, it must:
1. identify affected rights-holders and communities;
2. assess actual and potential human-rights impacts, including supply-chain and downstream impacts;
3. disclose material terms, ownership, provenance, pricing logic, and relevant conflicts of interest;
4. prevent, mitigate, and account for adverse impacts;
5. provide accessible complaint and remedy channels;
6. monitor outcomes and publish proportionate reports;
7. preserve the regulatory and policy space of states to meet their human-rights obligations.

Trade eligibility within the framework depends on compliance with these safeguards. A risk finding triggers proportionate prevention, mitigation, suspension, or remedy procedures; it does not authorize arbitrary punishment or collective discrimination.

## 4. Scope of logic commerce

The framework covers only defined logical assets and services, including:
- formal logic specifications and verified rule systems;
- algorithmic models, agents, and orchestration components;
- datasets and evidence packages where lawfully usable;
- verification, audit, and provenance services;
- logical software components, APIs, licenses, and interoperability standards;
- measured computational or service outputs where their measurement method is disclosed.

Each traded asset must carry an identity, version, creator/rights-holder record, provenance, license, verification status, known limitations, dependencies, and dispute process. A claim of verification must identify the verifier, method, evidence, date, and scope. Unverified claims must be labelled as such. No token or price is treated as proof of quality, truth, or public value.

## 5. Algorithmic governance limits

Algorithmic systems may administer defined workflows under published rules. They may not:
- create or amend constitutional rights by themselves;
- determine their own jurisdiction or expand their own authority;
- secretly change eligibility, prices, rankings, or sanctions;
- make unappealable final decisions about fundamental rights;
- infer legal guilt, identity, or entitlement without a lawful and reviewable basis;
- conceal evidence, decision rules, conflicts, or material system changes.

Every consequential system must provide: purpose limitation, authority record, data provenance, version control, explanation appropriate to the decision, audit logs, security controls, independent review, human appeal, rollback, and incident reporting.

## 6. Civic participation and the citizen application

The citizen application is the public interface to the constitutional framework. It must provide:
- readable access to the Constitution, amendments, explanatory materials, and impact reports;
- accessible proposals, public consultations, comments, and deliberation;
- verified membership/participation credentials with data minimization;
- transparent proposal thresholds, quorum rules, voting periods, and result publication;
- secure, independently auditable voting for decisions within the Institute's or participating network's lawful authority;
- an amendment tracker and public change history;
- complaint, appeal, and remedy submission;
- a personal dashboard for consent, data access, correction, and deletion requests where applicable.

The application must distinguish a civic consultation, an internal member vote, a binding contractual vote, and a public election. It must never present an internal poll as a state or national election. State elections may be supported only under the authority and legal procedures of the relevant election bodies.

## 7. Constitutional institutions and operational mechanisms

The Institute's framework should establish the following functionally separate bodies:

1. **Civic Constitutional Assembly** — drafts and proposes constitutional text and amendments; representation rules must be published and inclusive.
2. **Rights and Trade Commission** — conducts rights-impact review, trade safeguards, complaints intake, and remedy tracking.
3. **Algorithmic Governance Office** — maintains the registry of governed systems, authorization boundaries, risk tiers, and operational rules.
4. **Logic Standards and Verification Council** — defines asset schemas, verification protocols, provenance standards, and interoperability.
5. **Independent Audit and Appeals Chamber** — reviews contested decisions, audits system behavior, and orders correction within the framework's lawful powers.
6. **Public Ombuds Office** — assists individuals, small businesses, workers, and communities in complaints and appeals.
7. **Continental and Local Civic Chapters** — translate materials, organize participation, and report local impacts without overriding local law.
8. **Operations Secretariat** — coordinates services, records, budgets, delivery, and reporting; it does not control constitutional interpretation or appeals.

No single body may draft rules, execute them, and finally adjudicate its own disputes.

## 8. The AI-company cooperative as delivery infrastructure

The AI-company cooperative is an operational network that builds and maintains the tools needed by the Institute and participating communities. It is not the constitutional authority.

Its initial production portfolio:
- citizen application and multilingual access;
- constitutional document, amendment, and consultation platform;
- trade and human-rights due-diligence tools;
- logic-asset registry, licensing, provenance, and exchange;
- algorithmic-system registry and audit tooling;
- verification and testing services;
- secure identity and consent services;
- dispute, appeal, and case-management tools;
- public dashboards, data export, and interoperability;
- education and support services.

Companies may specialize in separate products. Shared standards and open interfaces prevent vendor lock-in. Procurement, ownership, conflicts of interest, and financial relationships must be disclosed.

## 9. Amendment and adoption

Amendments require public notice, accessible text, impact assessment, a defined deliberation period, transparent voting rules, and publication of results and dissenting opinions. Entrenched rights protections may not be reduced by ordinary administrative vote. Adoption by a state, treaty organization, or public authority requires its own valid legal process; the Institute cannot declare external legal recognition into existence.

## 10. Implementation stages

**Stage 1 — Founding instrument:** define membership, scope, rights floor, institutional separation, interim board limits, and adoption rules.

**Stage 2 — Constitutional draft:** prepare full articles, definitions, rights matrix, trade safeguards, algorithmic governance limits, and amendment procedures.

**Stage 3 — Operating institutions:** establish the commissions, ombuds, audit/appeals, standards council, and local/continental chapters with conflict-of-interest rules.

**Stage 4 — Citizen application MVP:** publish the Constitution, enable consultations, proposals, internal voting, complaints, appeals, and public audit records.

**Stage 5 — Logic commerce pilot:** launch a limited registry and exchange for verified logical assets with provenance, licensing, disclosures, and dispute handling.

**Stage 6 — Cross-border pilot:** invite voluntary organizations and businesses in multiple jurisdictions; conduct independent rights-impact review and publish results.

**Stage 7 — Legal adoption pathways:** seek formal adoption by participating entities and, where appropriate, competent public authorities using their lawful procedures.

## 11. Minimum acceptance tests

- A person can read and understand the rights, rules, and appeal route.
- A small supplier can disclose provenance and challenge a trade decision.
- A worker or community can submit a rights complaint without retaliation.
- A logic asset cannot be listed as verified without an evidence record.
- Every consequential algorithmic decision has a traceable rule, version, and appeal route.
- No internal vote is misrepresented as a public election.
- An auditor can reproduce a sample decision from preserved inputs, rules, and logs.
- A user can export their records and exercise applicable privacy rights.
- A vendor can be replaced without losing constitutional records or citizen access.
- Independent reviewers can publish adverse findings without approval from the audited operator.

## 12. Reference frameworks

This draft should be developed consistently with applicable international and domestic law and reviewed by qualified counsel in participating jurisdictions. Useful source frameworks include:
- UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights (UNGPs), including the state duty to protect, corporate responsibility to respect, and access to remedy.
- OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises on Responsible Business Conduct (2023), including human-rights due diligence.
- ILO MNE Declaration and fundamental labour standards.
- Applicable international human-rights treaties and domestic data-protection, consumer, labour, competition, trade, and election laws.

These frameworks inform the draft; they do not automatically confer legal status on this Constitution.
