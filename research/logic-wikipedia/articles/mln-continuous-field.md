## Definition
In the WANGA research hypothesis, M, L and N designate three continuous measurement/operator axes rather than three discrete software entities:
- M — Mind: a proposed observation/magnitude field including clarity, persistence and continuity measures.
- L — Logos: a proposed relational operator field expressing coupling between observations.
- N — Numerals: a proposed field of quantitative measurement/contribution.

These descriptions record the project's hypothesis. They do not establish a philosophical ontology or demonstrate cognition.

## Computational representation
The recovered MLN prototype uses observations (x, y, t, clarity_b, connectivity_c, stability_f, dM, dL, dN), kernel-smoothed fields BM/BL/BN, a symmetric L adjacency operator, a spectral-norm estimate and thresholded connectivity candidates.

The proposed diagnostic is E = abs((l + n) / m - phi), where phi = (1 + sqrt(5)) / 2. This is a configured research diagnostic, not a truth test. Scale normalization must be empirically justified before comparing E across datasets.

## Resonance and persistence
The archival source describes ten resonance patterns as five separate modes and five relational pairings. Software must not instantiate ten ontology objects merely because the source uses the word sphere. A cluster from one observation batch is only a candidate; persistence requires repeated-window evidence.

## Failure modes
- Invalid or incomparable M/L/N scales make the ratio meaningless.
- A high-connectivity block may come from sampling geometry rather than semantic coherence.
- Optimizing E may improve the diagnostic without improving task performance.
- A stable output hash proves reproducibility, not truth.
