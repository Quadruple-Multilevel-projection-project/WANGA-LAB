# Shadow language of thought — impression capture boundary

**Article ID:** shadow-language-impression-boundary  
**Category:** Research proposals / Cognitive architecture  
**Status:** PROPOSED · NOT YET FORMALIZED OR VERIFIED

## Definition

“Shadow language” is the proposed machine-readable representation of differences, contrasts, boundaries, relations, and transformations among recorded impressions (reshimot). It is a language about observable traces and their logical relations, not a capture of the intellect or of thought itself.

## Fundamental distinction

The research separates:

1. **Intellect / act of understanding** — the apprehending principle in the human account guiding this project.
2. **Logical thought-content** — abstract content and relations that can be defined and tested.
3. **Impressions** — records from which a machine can reconstruct selected logical moves.
4. **Shadow language** — the formal representation of distinctions among those impressions.

The machine network captures impressions and reconstructs logical sequences. It does not thereby acquire or demonstrate a human intellect.

## Human–machine asymmetry

In the project's human account, intellect is the principle that apprehends and retains understanding; impressions may be used during apprehension without every original trace needing permanent preservation. The machine has no presumed intellect in this architecture, so it must preserve impressions, provenance, transformations, and replay records to reconstruct selected logical moves.

This is a conceptual research distinction, not an established empirical theory of human cognition or AI consciousness.

## Five matters and four impression channels

The project refers to five distinct matters involved in abstract thought. Their exact source-grounded definitions have not yet been provided. They are therefore recorded as `FIVE_MATTERS_UNSPECIFIED`. They must not be equated with the four machine-side impression channels:

- I1: external / first-order source impressions;
- I2: external / second-order relations;
- I3: internal / cognitive computational representations;
- I4: internal / ontological computational representations.

“Internal” refers to machine-side representation, not subjective experience.

## Experimental claims that can be tested

The network can test which impressions were captured, which distinctions were preserved or lost, whether logical transitions can be replayed deterministically, and whether reconstructions survive source checks and counterexamples. It cannot infer subjective experience or intellect from successful replay.

For the planned 175 × 14 = 2,450 chapter-term matrix, each unit should preserve source hashes, exact locators, channel records, transformations, nulls/conflicts, replay results, and visual snapshots. The 2,450 count is a planned matrix, not proof that 2,450 Excel files have been ingested.

## Relation types

Candidate relation vocabulary: `DISTINGUISHES`, `CONTRASTS_WITH`, `PRESUPPOSES`, `DEPENDS_ON`, `COMPOSES_WITH`, `CONTRADICTS`, `INVARIANT_UNDER`, `LOST_IN_TRANSFORMATION`, `RECONSTRUCTED_FROM`, `UNRESOLVED_AGAINST`.

Every relation requires provenance and a validation state. Difference is not automatically contradiction.
