## 1. Executive thesis

The Logic Economy is a proposed economy of reusable, task-specific logical assets and the products, services, integrations, and ventures built from them.

The economic asset is not an abstract claim that logic itself is money. The asset is a documented and reusable logical capability with:
- identifiable source and provenance;
- a defined task and domain;
- explicit inputs, outputs, rules, constraints, and boundaries;
- configuration and version history;
- reproducible tests, counterexamples, and verification status;
- evidence of operational utility and, eventually, customer willingness to pay.

**Core principle:** Logic is the productive asset; verified utility is the basis for commercial value. A currency or financial instrument is a separate legal and economic design question, not proof of value by itself.

This project connects WANGA-LAB's Logic Mine and Logic Boxes productization path with the Logical Islands Economy proposal in AI231 / QUALIA.

## 3. Product and value model

### 3.1 Product hierarchy

1. **Logic Unit** — a source-linked logical primitive, relation, rule, constraint, or derivation.
2. **Logic Package** — a versioned bundle of compatible Logic Units for a bounded task.
3. **Logic Box** — a packaged workflow with input/output contract, gates, failure states, tests, and documentation.
4. **Logic Bundle** — a tested composition of multiple Logic Boxes.
5. **Agent Capability** — a runtime-adapted package discoverable and executable by an agent under defined permissions.
6. **Integrated Solution** — customer-specific workflow, deployment, monitoring, and support.
7. **Research Asset** — a preserved logic structure whose future application may not yet be known; not automatically a commercial product.

## 5. Market entry and customer problem

### 5.1 Initial customer segments to validate

- Enterprise AI teams that need source-grounded claim checks and bounded action controls.
- Financial and insurance institutions that need traceable policy, evidence, and exception review.
- Legal, compliance, procurement, and audit workflows that compare obligations, evidence, and approvals.
- AI-agent developers who need reusable, model-independent logic capabilities.
- Research and engineering groups that need source-linked formalization and reproducible logic packages.

These are candidate segments, not validated customers or proof of market demand.

### 5.2 Initial problems to sell against

- Claims that exceed or contradict their source.
- Missing evidence and unclear provenance.
- Scope violations between a task instruction and a proposed action.
- Conflicting rules, exceptions, and conditions in documents.
- Repeated manual review of the same logical task.
- Agent workflows that lack deterministic checks and explainable failure states.

### 5.3 Initial commercial wedge

Start with one bounded, measurable workflow rather than selling a universal logic engine. The recommended first commercial wedge is source-grounded claim and document consistency review, supported by the AI Logic Auditor demonstrator and the Evidence Gatekeeper specification.

The pilot must measure baseline review time, detection quality, false positives, missed findings, provenance completeness, human correction rate, and customer willingness to pay.

## 6. Revenue model hypotheses

Potential revenue streams, to validate separately:
- one-time purchase or licence for a reusable Logic Box;
- subscription for updates, version access, and maintained test suites;
- paid configuration for a defined customer task;
- integration and deployment fees;
- usage-based execution charges where compute or runtime costs apply;
- custom logic package development;
- enterprise support, audit evidence, and maintenance agreements;
- licensing to agent platforms or runtime providers.

Do not publish prices, revenue forecasts, margins, or conversion rates as established facts until supported by cost models and customer evidence.

## 7. Logic Economy and the wider Logical Islands proposal

The Logical Islands Economy document proposes a broader circulation model:
1. protect foundational logical infrastructure;
2. commercialise products, services, software, integrations, research outputs, and operating ventures enabled by it;
3. validate customer utility, demand, safety, and unit economics;
4. pay documented operating costs, taxes, contractual partner compensation, and required reserves;
5. allocate approved portions of distributable profit to reinvestment, the Logical Islands Fund, and public advancement.

The proposed Logical Currency, Logical Stock Market, and Logical Islands Fund are not represented as launched or authorised financial products. No token issuance, reserve backing, redemption, exchange, share offering, guaranteed return, sovereign status, or regulatory exemption is claimed.

Before any financial launch, obtain jurisdiction-specific legal, regulatory, tax, custody, accounting, AML/CFT, consumer-protection, securities, and independent-audit review. The economic value of a Logic Package must be demonstrated through utility, provenance, quality, demand, and repeatable use—not by issuing a token.

## 9. Repository integration rules

Do not move or merge code as the first step. Establish a cross-repository registry with:
- repository URL and owner;
- canonical project ID;
- role: CORE / SOURCE / PRODUCT / EVIDENCE / RUNTIME / ECONOMY / REFERENCE;
- original author/upstream/fork/mirror status;
- licence and IP status;
- exact dependency or interface;
- evidence for the relationship;
- development and verification status;
- maintenance owner and next action.

A repository is connected only when there is at least one verified relationship: a documented interface, dependency, test fixture, source reference, shared schema, release artefact, or maintained cross-link. A list entry or hyperlink alone is not technical integration.

- Keep the currency, fund, and securities proposals separate from operating revenue.

- Output: reviewed legal/financial design; no launch before all required gates pass.
