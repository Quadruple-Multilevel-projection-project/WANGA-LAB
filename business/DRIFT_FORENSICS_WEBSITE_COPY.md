### AI Drift Forensics
**Measure what changed. Reconstruct how it changed. Preserve the evidence.**

We build evidence-first infrastructure for detecting, reconstructing, and verifying changes in AI reasoning and model behavior.

## What we do

AI systems can change their outputs because definitions, premises, criteria, relations, operations, references, abstractions, or context have changed. A final answer alone does not show where that change occurred.

Our drift-forensics approach reconstructs the derivation path:

**Source → Primitives → Operations → Relations → Transformations → Abstraction → Output**

We compare reproducible runs, preserve evidence, identify change points, and produce an auditable chain from input to result.

## Core workflow

**Baseline → Observation → Detection → Evidence Preservation → Reconstruction → Causal/Dependency Analysis → Attribution → Risk Quantification → Intervention → Verification**

## Evidence-first architecture

Every material transformation can carry a receipt containing provenance, input/output hashes, operation metadata, source references, timestamp, and verification state.

We distinguish VERIFIED, CONDITIONALLY_VERIFIED, NOT_YET_VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED, REPRESENTATION_ONLY, SIMULATION_MODEL, and ENGINEERING_ASSUMPTION.

A hash proves integrity of recorded data; it does not by itself prove semantic truth.

## Research boundary

We do not claim direct access to subjective consciousness. We measure reproducible changes in representations and derivation paths available to a system.

Computational models, simulations, and ontological mappings are kept separate from claims about physical implementation.

## Why it matters

For high-stakes AI use, the important forensic question is not only what answer was produced, but: What input was used? Which primitives were activated? What operations were performed? Which relation changed? Where did the derivation path diverge? What evidence can be replayed?
